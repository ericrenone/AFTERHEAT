# AFTERHEAT

**The Residual-Function Framework for Rebuilding What a Rupture Removed**

> A tradition is a function wearing a mechanism. When the mechanism is replaced, the materials are forgotten, and the makers are gone, the function is what remains — and the function is the only sound thing to search for.

Afterheat is a method for recovering, reconstructing, and honestly claiming traditions that survived a break in their own continuity. It was built from a single case: what Sichuan food tasted like before the chili pepper, and why that question is harder — and more useful — than it looks.

## 1. Why this framework exists

Ask almost anyone what Sichuan food tastes like and they will say *hot*. Ask what it tasted like five hundred years ago and most will assume: the same, only older. Both answers are wrong in an instructive way.

The chili pepper is a plant of the Americas. It is first recorded in China in 1591, in a Hangzhou connoisseur's catalogue of garden curiosities, as an ornamental. It was first eaten, as far as the written record shows, in the Guizhou highlands in 1721 — as a substitute for salt. It is first mentioned in Sichuan in 1749. Every famous Sichuan dish postdates it; the most famous were invented in the last 160 years, and some in the last 40.

More unsettling: the Sichuan that eats this food is itself recent. The province lost as much as ninety percent of its people between the 1640s and the 1680s — to war, massacre, famine, epidemic, and tigers — and was refilled over the following century by migrants from Hubei, Hunan, Guangdong, Fujian, Jiangxi, and beyond. By the 1720s, roughly three-quarters of the people of Sichuan were non-natives. The cuisine is not an inheritance. It is a reconstruction, assembled by newcomers, from whatever was available, on top of an appetite that was already there.

And yet a fourth-century gazetteer says that the people of Shu *prized flavor and loved pungency and fragrance*. More than a thousand years before the chili, something in this place wanted heat.

That is the puzzle the framework is built to handle. It is not a culinary puzzle only. The same structure appears wherever a practice outlives its own continuity: a craft whose materials went extinct, a liturgy whose language died, a codebase whose authors left, an institution rebuilt after a collapse, a scientific technique whose original instrument no longer exists. In every one of these, people make the same two errors — they read the current mechanism back into the deep past, or they conclude that because the mechanism is new the tradition is fake. Afterheat is the discipline that refuses both.

## 2. The core claim

A tradition is a stack of layers that reset at different rates:

| Layer | Example in the Sichuan case | Minimum time to reset |
|---|---|---|
| **Function** | The appetite for pungent, fragrant, warming food in a damp basin | Centuries to millennia |
| **Mechanism** | The specific agent of pungency: prickly-ash fruit, ginger, mustard, pepper, chili | Decades to centuries |
| **Material** | The cultivar, the fermentation, the oil, the salt | Decades |
| **Makers** | The cooks, households, migrants, restaurants | One catastrophe; otherwise centuries |
| **Recipe** | Named dishes, ratios, sequences | Years to a century |

A rupture — a catastrophe, a lost text, a dead language, an extinct supply chain — resets the fast layers unevenly: some components cross it, most do not. The slow layer survives whole. Call that surviving slow layer the **afterheat**: the function that is still felt after the mechanism that delivered it is gone. **Afterheat's claim is that this surviving function is both the best evidence of what came before and the only sound search key for rebuilding it.** Rebuilding from the fast layers — reproducing a recipe, recovering a named ingredient — without first isolating the function produces a counterfeit or an error.

A reconstruction is **sound** when it meets five conditions:

| Condition | Question it answers | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Stratified** | Does every component carry a date, a place, and an entry route? | A timeline with no undated element |
| **Function-dated** | Is the function attested independently of, and before, the current mechanism? | A pre-rupture source that names the function without naming the mechanism |
| **Mechanism-mapped** | Are substitutes matched by what they do, not what they are called? | A mapping at the level of effect (receptor, volatility, timing, yield) |
| **Falsifiable in the mouth** | Was a sensory prediction written before the test, and could it have failed? | A prediction and a breaker, both on paper before the fire is lit |
| **Rupture-honest** | Is the discontinuity named, rather than smoothed over? | The claim states what was lost, what was reconstructed, and what cannot be known |

A reconstruction that meets fewer is a *plausible story*. Plausible stories are useful — every real reconstruction starts as one — but they must not be mistaken for the finished thing, and the central discipline of the framework is refusing to let a plausible story feel like a lineage.

**What this adds.** The layered-speed picture is not new: it is pace layering in the sense Stewart Brand gave it, and the *longue durée* in Braudel's. The discipline of cooking a hypothesis and recording what broke is experimental archaeology, practiced by historians who cook. Afterheat adds three things to that practice: an **entry-function** field, because mechanisms arrive through a different door than the one they later stand in; a **found/made** test, because a storable agent standing in for a procedural one changes the craft and not merely the plant; and the rule that the **breaker targets a clause of the function**, not the chemistry and not the history. Those three are the framework's own.

## 3. The case: Sichuan before the chili

This section is the evidence. Each stage below names the findings it rests on.

### 3.1 The pungent function is ancient and is attested without the chili

The *Huayang Guozhi* (華陽國志), compiled by Chang Qu in the mid-fourth century and the oldest surviving regional gazetteer in China, says the people of Shu *prized flavor* (尚滋味) and *loved pungency and fragrance* (好辛香). The word for pungency, *xin* (辛), is one of the five canonical flavors and predates every New World plant. It covered ginger, Sichuan pepper, garlic, scallion, mustard, and the prickly-ash fruit discussed below — a family of sensations, not one burn.

Sichuan pepper (*Zanthoxylum*, 花椒 *huajiao*) is the oldest attested spice of the region: archaeological, genetic, and linguistic evidence converge on its use in western Sichuan by at least the fourth millennium BCE, although the earliest Sichuan find is not yet directly dated and the evidence does not settle whether it was gathered or grown. It appears in the *Book of Songs*. It numbs rather than burns, a sensation (*ma*, 麻) with no equivalent in European cooking. Ginger is named in the *Analects* — ginger was never cleared from Confucius's table (不撤薑食) — and in Han-era tomb inventories. Mustard sauce with raw fish is prescribed in the *Liji*, the Han compilation of Zhou ritual. Cassia and angelica are attested before the Han; the native small garlic (小蒜) is pre-Han, the large garlic (大蒜) a Han-era import from Central Asia. Fermented soybeans (豉) are in the Mawangdui tomb of the second century BCE.

So the function — a food culture organized around pungency, fragrance, and warming — is documented more than a millennium before capsicum existed in Asia.

### 3.2 The classical pungents formed a trinity, and one of them is now forgotten

A third-century text, Zhou Chu's *Fengtu Ji* (風土記), already names the "three aromatics" (三香): Sichuan pepper, ginger, and 欓 — the fruit of *Zanthoxylum ailanthoides*, a prickly-ash cousin of Sichuan pepper, known in later texts as 食茱萸 *shi zhuyu* and conventionally mistranslated into English as "food cornel" (true cornels are *Cornus*; this plant is a prickly ash). Its piquant fruit served as a local stand-in for a hot pepper before the Columbian exchange. Song Qi's eleventh-century catalogue of the products of Shu, the *Yibu Fangwu Lüeji* (益部方物略記), says the people of Shu dropped a grain or two into every meat broth, and that a moment later the fragrance filled the bowl. Li Shizhen's *Bencao Gangmu* (completed 1578) records the Shu name for the fruit, *aizi* (艾子), and the chili-like condiment oil pressed from it — *aiyou* (艾油), also called *lami you* (辣米油), "pungent-grain oil" — used in food.

That plant did not merely decline when the chili arrived; it largely vanished from the cuisine within a century or two of the chili's adoption, so completely that most Sichuanese today have never heard of it. This is the clearest demonstration in the case of a mechanism overwritten by a successor that served the same function better, cheaper, and in more forms — and of how thoroughly a replaced mechanism can be forgotten. This document keeps the conventional English name "cornel" for it, with that warning given once.

### 3.3 The imported pungents — black pepper, long pepper, mustard — were already in the Ming kitchen

Black pepper came from India by roughly the third century: Sima Biao's *Xu Han Shu* lists it among the products of India, and Zhang Hua's *Bowu Zhi* describes a pepper-infused wine. It was costly — the Tang chancellor Yuan Zai, executed in 777, was found to have hoarded eight hundred *shi* of it — and it remained a marker of wealth. Ming-era cookbooks nonetheless used it alongside Sichuan pepper, ginger, garlic, and mustard.

Mustard is the most interesting of these, because its heat is not stored in the seed; it is *manufactured* at the moment of use. Ground mustard seed is inert until water activates an enzyme that releases a volatile isothiocyanate. Prolonged heat kills the enzyme; cold slows the reaction; left too long, the volatile escapes. The Yuan household encyclopedia *Jujia Biyong Shilei Quanji* (居家必用事類全集), followed by Ming cookbooks such as Song Xu's *Songshi Yangsheng Bu* (1504), describes the craft in detail: grind two-year-aged mustard seed fine, mix it with water, scald it briefly three to five times to pour off the yellow, bitter liquid, then cover it and set it on the cold ground; after a while the pungency rises, and the paste is loosened with mild vinegar. A Ming cook who wanted a sharp heat made it fresh, by technique, from a plant that would be flat an hour later.

This matters for reconstruction: the pre-chili heat was *procedural*. It lived in method and in a schedule, not in a jar.

### 3.4 The chili arrived early, was ignored for a century, and entered the diet through a different function

The first Chinese record of the chili is Gao Lian's *Zunsheng Bajian* (遵生八箋, 1591), where it is listed among ornamental plants: growing in clusters, white flowers, fruit shaped like a worn brush-tip, "pungent in flavor, red in color, very fine to look at" (味辣色紅，甚可觀). The same book contains Gao Lian's own treatise on food and drink — a complete pre-chili pantry of pungents. The chili and its predecessors sit in the same volume, unconnected. The plant was named *fanjiao* (番椒), "foreign pepper," by analogy to the native pepper it would later displace; in Sichuan it is still called *haijiao* (海椒), "sea pepper."

For over a century it stayed in the flowerbed. The first record of anyone eating it is the *Sizhou Fuzhi* (思州府志) of 1721, a prefectural gazetteer from Guizhou, which notes that the local Miao used it *in place of salt* (土苗用以代鹽). Salt was a state monopoly, taxed and expensive; the highlands were poor and remote. The chili was adopted not for heat but as a free, home-grown substitute for the one seasoning the body cannot do without. Only afterward did it migrate into the role of pungent.

Historians of the chili in China stress that no one deliberately imported it; it arrived at multiple points, by sea and overland, under a dozen local names, and a national name (辣椒 *lajiao*) did not settle until the late nineteenth century. Its first appearance in a recipe collection is around 1790, in the *Tiaoding Ji* (調鼎集), a compilation whose own date is uncertain.

### 3.5 Sichuan took it last, and then took it entirely

The first mention of the chili in Sichuan is the *Dayi Xianzhi* (大邑縣志) of 1749, a county gazetteer. Early-nineteenth-century gazetteers show it widely grown; by the late nineteenth century it was the defining flavor. In the interval, the Sichuan pepper that most of China had largely abandoned after the sixteenth century was retained in Sichuan and paired with the newcomer, producing the compound sensation *mala* (麻辣, numbing-hot) that now reads as the province's signature. The signature is a hybrid of the oldest mechanism in the region and the newest.

### 3.6 The dishes are young

| Dish | Attested origin | Age |
|---|---|---|
| Mapo tofu | Chen Xingsheng eatery, Wanfu Bridge, Chengdu, 1862 by convention; listed among the city's famous eateries in the 1909–10 *Chengdu Tonglan* | ~160 years |
| Kung Pao chicken | Named for Ding Baozhen, governor-general of Sichuan 1876–1886 | ~140 years |
| Dandan noodles | Zigong street vendors, 1841 by convention | ~185 years |
| Pixian doubanjiang | Chen family of Pixian (by company tradition, migrants from Fujian in the Kangxi era); earliest documented firm 1803 (順天號), branded 益豐和 1853 | ~170–220 years documented; ~330 by tradition |
| Chongqing hotpot | First described in the 1940s by Li Jieren as a dish of the 1920s–30s Jiangbei dockside; earlier "Daoguang-era" precursors are asserted but undocumented | ~100 years |
| Fuqi feipian | Chengdu, 1930s | ~90 years |
| Water-boiled beef (shuizhu) | Zigong salt-well workers, early twentieth century | ~90 years |
| Laziji (chili chicken) | Geleshan, Chongqing, 1990s | ~30 years |

Fu Chongju's *Chengdu Tonglan* of 1909–10 catalogues 1,328 dishes and is the earliest comprehensive listing of Chengdu's dishes and eateries. The term "Sichuan cuisine" as a named regional school is a late-Qing and Republican construction.

### 3.7 The people are young too

The late Ming and the Ming–Qing transition reduced Sichuan from several million people (the 1578 register counts 3.1 million; estimates run higher) to a remnant. Zhang Xianzhong's regime (1644–1647) burned Chengdu and is credited — by Qing historiography, which had reason to blame him — with mass killings; continuing warfare into the 1660s, famine, epidemic, tiger predation, and the Qing conquest itself did the rest. Estimates of the loss run as high as ninety percent; Qing registers of the 1660s count about sixteen thousand taxable adult males in the whole province.

The Qing response was *Huguang tian Sichuan* (湖廣填四川), "Huguang fills Sichuan": tax holidays, seed grain, livestock, free land, and credit toward promotion for officials who recruited settlers, to draw people from Huguang (Hubei and Hunan), Guangdong, Fujian, Jiangxi, and Shaanxi. By the 1720s the province was seventy to eighty percent non-native; a century later, as much as eighty-five percent. Guild halls in Chongqing still carry the names of the migrants' provinces.

The modern Sichuan population, dialect, and food culture date from this refilling. The migrant inventions are the proof: doubanjiang is, by its founding legend, a migrant family's accident with local broad beans and the new chili; the dock-worker hotpot is a Chongqing port phenomenon; the bean-and-chili pantry that now reads as timelessly local was assembled by people who had been in the province for one or two generations.

### 3.8 The sensations are chemically distinct, and the substitution was not neutral

What the tongue calls "heat" is several different signals:

| Agent | Source | Principal receptor | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capsaicin | Chili | TRPV1 (heat-pain channel) | Non-volatile, oil-soluble, slow-building, long-lasting burn in the mouth |
| Piperine | Black and long pepper | TRPV1, with TRPA1 | Warm, moderate, slow to clear |
| Gingerol → shogaol | Ginger (shogaol forms on drying and heating) | TRPV1, with TRPA1 | Warm, cleaner, fades faster than capsaicin |
| Allyl isothiocyanate | Mustard, released enzymatically on wetting | TRPA1 (irritant channel) | Volatile, rises into the nose, violent and brief, destroyed by boiling |
| Allicin | Garlic, released enzymatically on crushing | TRPA1 and TRPV1 | Sharp, fades with cooking |
| Cinnamaldehyde | Cassia | TRPA1 | Warming, aromatic |
| Hydroxy-α-sanshool | Sichuan pepper and the "cornel" | Two-pore potassium channels (KCNK3/9/18), with TRPV1/TRPA1 | Tingling, numbing, vibratory — not a burn |

The pre-chili pantry was weighted toward the volatile, nasal, enzymatically-made TRPA1 heats (mustard, garlic) and the tingling of *Zanthoxylum*, with black pepper and ginger supplying a moderate TRPV1 warmth. The chili moved the center of mass to a non-volatile, oil-borne, long-lasting TRPV1 burn that could be stored, dried, fermented, infused, and dosed at will.

That shift is why the substitution was so complete and why it is not reversible by simply "leaving the chili out." The chili did not replace a flavor; it replaced a *delivery system*. The function — pungency, fragrance, warming — is continuous. The mechanism and the sensation are not.

### 3.9 Pre-chili recipes do not say how

The Ming sources that survive — Song Xu's *Songshi Yangsheng Bu* (1504), Gao Lian's food treatise within the *Zunsheng Bajian* (1591), the earlier *Wushi Zhongkui Lu* (Song–Yuan), *Jujia Biyong* (Yuan), and *Yinshan Zhengyao* (1330) — give ingredients, sequence, and occasionally a rough quantity. They rarely give ratios, times, or temperatures; the mustard procedure above, with its "after a while," is as precise as they get. They were written for readers who already knew the hand-skills and who needed a reminder of combination, not instruction in technique. Anyone cooking from them must supply the missing procedure from somewhere: surviving regional practice, the chemistry of the ingredients, or trial. The recipe is a sketch; the function is the constraint that lets you fill it in.

## 4. What the case teaches

The nine findings above collapse into six lessons, and each becomes a stage of the framework.

1. **The function outlives the mechanism.** A fourth-century preference survived a change of plants, a change of population, and a change of chemistry. (→ Stage II; §11)
2. **Replaced mechanisms are forgotten.** The cornel was among the pungents of Shu for well over a thousand years and had largely vanished from the cuisine within a century or two of the chili. The record of a mechanism is not preserved by the people who stopped using it. (→ Stage I)
3. **Availability is not adoption; adoption is not for the obvious reason.** The chili was present for a century before anyone is recorded eating it, and when they did it was as salt, not spice. Mechanisms enter through whatever function is most starved. (→ Stage I, Stage III)
4. **Substitutes must be matched by effect, not by name.** "Pepper" names three unrelated plants acting on three different receptors. Reconstructing by vocabulary produces the wrong sensation with the right word. (→ Stage III)
5. **The sketch must be completed by test, not by confidence.** The texts omit the procedure; fluent interpretation of a gap is still a gap. (→ Stage IV)
6. **The rupture must be stated, not smoothed.** Modern Sichuan is a migrant reconstruction on an older function. Saying so is more credible than claiming either an unbroken line or a fiction. (→ Stage V)

The five stages answer the five conditions of §2 one for one: Strata makes a reconstruction *stratified*, Appetite makes it *function-dated*, Pantry makes it *mechanism-mapped*, Fire makes it *falsifiable in the mouth*, Table makes it *rupture-honest*. The stages are sequential for any single reconstruction, but a practitioner has many in flight. The stratigraphy (Stage I) is the persistent artifact; everything else operates on entries in it.

## 6. Stage I — Strata

### Date every layer, its place, and its door

**Principle.** Nothing enters the reconstruction without a date, a place, and a door. A component with no date is assumed new for the purposes of search — it does not count toward coverage until it is sourced — because the direction of error is asymmetric: traditions are routinely claimed older than they are and almost never younger.

**Why this stage comes first.** Most retellings of the Sichuan case fail here. The chili reads as ancient because it is ubiquitous (§3.4, §3.5); the cornel reads as nonexistent because it is absent (§3.2). Both readings come from treating the present pantry as the historical one. Stratigraphy is the correction: lay the components out in time and place, and watch the pantry change under you. The makers are a layer too (§3.7): where the population was replaced, surviving hand-skill is not a primary source for anything before the replacement.

**The stratum card.** Every component gets one:

```
COMPONENT: ________________

First attestation     Earliest dated source in this tradition, with the source named.
Place attested        Where that source locates it — region, not polity. Regional and
                      national attestations get separate lines.
Entry route           How it arrived (native, trade, migration, accident).
Entry function        What it was first used FOR — which may differ from its later role.
Adoption lag          Time between availability and use, at the place in question.
Displaced             What it replaced, and whether the replaced thing is still remembered.
Still present?        Yes / No / Residual (e.g. retained only in a hybrid).
Carrier across        For any component or function claimed to cross the rupture:
the rupture           place / remnant population / migrants' source traditions / texts / unknown.
Confidence            Which of the above rests on a primary source, and which on convention.
```

**Practices.**

- Date the *function* as well as the mechanisms, and give it a card of its own. The *Huayang Guozhi* line is a stratum card for an appetite — and its "carrier across the rupture" field is the single most important blank in the Sichuan case (§7).
- Record the entry function separately from the current function. The chili entered as salt. Doubanjiang entered, by its legend, as a way to save spoiled broad beans. Hotpot entered as dock food. Most components arrive through a door other than the one they now stand in.
- Keep regional and national attestations apart. The chili's adoption lag is about 130 years nationally (1591 → 1721) and about 160 years for Sichuan (1591 → 1749); the two numbers answer different questions.
- Look for what the current pantry displaced. A displaced component is usually unrecorded by its successors; look in materia medica, product catalogues, gazetteers, and ritual texts rather than cookbooks. The cornel survives in Song Qi's product catalogue and Li Shizhen's pharmacopoeia, not in any Sichuan cookbook.
- Distinguish convention from attestation. "1862" for mapo tofu is a founding date carried by later tradition; "1909" is a primary listing. Mark the difference on the card.
- Treat the makers as a layer with its own card. If the population was replaced (Sichuan, 1640s–1720s), every component attested only after the replacement is a migrant component until shown otherwise.

**Exit criterion.** A timeline in which every component, the function, and the makers carry a date, a place, an entry route, and a confidence mark, and in which the rupture (if any) is drawn as a line that components either cross or do not, with a carrier named for each that crosses.

## 7. Stage II — Appetite

### Isolate and freeze the function the slow layer kept

**Principle.** Strip the mechanisms away and state what the tradition was *for*, in terms that would still be true if every current ingredient disappeared. Then freeze the statement, with a date, before any mechanism table is opened.

**Why.** The function is the search key (§3.1). "Chili-hot" retrieves only the chili. "Pungent, fragrant, warming, sharp enough to cut rich food" retrieves mustard, ginger, cornel, pepper, garlic, cassia, and Sichuan pepper — the whole pre-chili pantry — and would retrieve a future substitute as well. The function also explains the adoption pattern (§3.4, §3.5): the chili entered where salt was scarce, and once Sichuan took it, it displaced every incumbent pungent because it served the existing appetite in more forms.

**Why freeze it.** The quiet way fluency enters a reconstruction is not through invented ratios but through the reshaping of what the dish was *for*, at the moment a substitution needs licensing. A function clause added after the pantry has been opened, to make a candidate pass, is not evidence; it is tailoring. The statement is therefore written and dated before Stage III. Any later amendment is permitted only if it is logged with the source that licenses it (see the worked example, §17, for an amendment and its source).

**The function statement.** One line, no mechanism nouns:

> "Deliver a sharp, aromatic, warming stimulus that cuts fat, at household cost, in a form that can be made daily."

Each clause is a constraint that must disqualify something. *Household cost* rules out black pepper as the primary agent in a poor household. *Made daily* admits mustard (procedural, fresh) and chili (dried, storable) alike and rules out nothing until it is tightened to "made fresh for the meal" — which the Yuan and Ming mustard manuals (§3.3) license, and which the worked example logs as an amendment.

**The carrier.** A function that is claimed to persist across a rupture persists *by something*. The Sichuan case offers at least four candidates: the place (a damp basin, rich in pork and starch, that asks the same of any diet); a remnant population that survived the collapse; the migrants' own source cuisines (Hunan and Guizhou were already pungent); and texts. "The land kept making the same demand" is a hypothesis about the carrier, not a finding, and it is the part of the slow clock most likely to be wrong. Name the carrier you are assuming on the function's stratum card, and say in Stage V that it is assumed.

**Practices.**

- Write the function from a pre-rupture source if one exists. If it does not, write it from the environment and mark it as inferred: what does this place, diet, economy, and climate ask of a food?
- Rewrite the function three ways. "Make food taste strong" retrieves salt and ferments; "stimulate the trigeminal nerve" retrieves every pungent and also menthol and carbonation; "warm the body against damp" retrieves medicine as well as spice. Each retrieves a different candidate set.
- Separate the function from the sensation. The pre-chili sensation (brief, nasal, tingling) and the post-chili sensation (deep, lasting, oral) are different; the function is the same. A reconstruction that reproduces the function but not the modern sensation is correctly scoped, not failed.
- Note which functions were starved. The chili entered Guizhou through the salt function. A starved function is the door the next mechanism will come through.

**Exit criterion.** A dated function statement with no mechanism nouns, attested or marked as inferred, with a named carrier, that disqualifies at least one historically available agent — a function statement that rules nothing out is not yet a constraint — and that a specialist in a different tradition could read and recognize as something their own tradition also serves.

## 8. Stage III — Pantry

### Map what each agent does, not what it is called

**Principle.** A substitute is valid when it reproduces the *effect*, and effect is measured at the level of mechanism — receptor, volatility, timing, yield, storability, cost — never at the level of vocabulary.

**Why most reconstructions fail here.** The Chinese word 椒 (*jiao*) covers Sichuan pepper, black pepper, and chili — three plants, three chemistries, three receptors (§3.8). 辛 covers every pungent. A reconstruction that works from the words will put the right name on the wrong sensation and never know. The only defense is a mapping that strips the names.

**The mechanism table.** For each candidate agent in each role:

```
ROLE: ______________     FUNCTION CLAUSE SERVED: ______________

Agent          Active principle   Receptor/effect   Volatile?  Onset   Duration  Storable?  Cost   Made or found?
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
(candidate)    ___________        ___________       Y/N        ___     ___       Y/N        ___    made / found
(incumbent)    ___________        ___________       Y/N        ___     ___       Y/N        ___    made / found

Unmapped properties of the incumbent that the candidate lacks: ______________
Unmapped properties of the candidate that the incumbent lacked: ______________
```

**Three mapping tests, applied in order.**

1. **The strip test.** Describe the incumbent's effect with every plant name replaced by its active principle and receptor. Describe the candidate the same way. If the descriptions coincide, the substitution is at the level of mechanism. If they coincide only on the word "hot," it is at the level of vocabulary. (Chili → "non-volatile TRPV1 agonist, oil-soluble, minutes-long." Mustard → "volatile TRPA1 agonist, water-released, seconds-long, nasal." These do not coincide. Black pepper → "non-volatile TRPV1/TRPA1 agonist, moderate, minutes-long." Closer. Ginger → "TRPV1 agonist, moderate, intensified by drying." Closer still, and ancient in the region.)
2. **The procedure test.** Identify whether the effect is *found* in the ingredient or *made* by technique. Mustard heat is made (enzyme + water + temperature + rest). Chili heat is found (dried, infused, fermented, it persists). A reconstruction that uses a found agent to stand in for a made one has changed the craft, not just the plant, and must say so.
3. **The inertness test.** Everything the incumbent does that the candidate does not must be shown to be inert for the function *as frozen in Stage II*. The chili's storability was *not* inert — it is why doubanjiang, chili oil, and dried-chili dishes exist, and none of those forms were available to a mustard-and-cornel kitchen. A reconstruction that swaps chili for cornel in mapo tofu has not reconstructed a pre-chili dish; it has made a modern dish with an archaic garnish.

**Mapping at multiple levels.** Pungency is one role. A dish also needs a salt role, a fat role, a fermented-umami role, a sour role, an aromatic role. The pre-chili kitchen had all of these — soy ferments since the Han, vinegar from antiquity, salt from Sichuan's own brine wells — and some of them, not the chili, are the real continuity between Ming and modern Sichuan tables. Keep a separate mechanism table for each role.

**Exit criterion.** A mechanism table per role in which every candidate is characterized by effect, the made/found distinction is marked, and every property the candidate fails to reproduce is listed with a judgment of whether it matters for the frozen function.

## 9. Stage IV — Fire

### Cook the test

**Principle.** A reconstruction you cannot name a failing taste for is a reconstruction you do not understand. The difference between a plausible story and a result is a sensory prediction that could have been wrong and was not.

**Why.** Historical cooking is especially exposed to the fluency trap (§3.9): a confident interpreter can fill every gap in a Ming recipe with a reasonable guess, the dish will come out *delicious*, and deliciousness will be mistaken for correctness. The only countermeasure is to decide, before cooking, what the dish must do and what would show it does not.

**What the breaker must target.** The breaker is written against a clause of the frozen function — "cuts fat," "sharp within seconds," "made at household cost." A breaker written against the chemistry ("if the heat persists, the mustard is not the working agent") is an *execution check* on the made step: it confirms the cook activated the mustard, which is necessary, but it cannot fail the mapping, because textbook chemistry was never in doubt. Record execution checks, but do not count them as breakers.

**The fire record.**

```
RECONSTRUCTION: ______________     FUNCTION CLAUSE(S) TARGETED: ______________

Prediction      If the mapping is sound, then when I ____, the result will ____
                (e.g. "cut the fat of braised pork at least as well as the chili control").
Breaker         If instead ____, the mapping fails at clause ____
                (e.g. "if blinded tasters rate the fat-cutting below the control, 'cuts fat' fails").
Execution check The observation that confirms the made step was performed
                (e.g. "nasal heat within 15 s, gone by 2 min; absent in the boiled bowl").
Regime          The prediction holds only when ____ — including the schedule
                (e.g. "mustard wetted below ~50°C, rested 10–15 min, served within 30 min").
Out-of-regime   One deliberate run outside the regime: ____
                (e.g. "boil the mustard; the sharp heat should collapse").
Control         The reference the result is compared against, and whether tasters are blinded.
Smallest cook   The minimum preparation that produces the observation.
Budget          ____ hours. Hard stop.
Observation     (filled in after)
Verdict         per claim — structural / sensory / historical / contextual:
                holds / holds in regime ___ only / fails at ___
```

**Rules for the smallest cook.**

- It must exercise the *made* step if the mapping identified one. If the mustard is not actually activated by technique in the test, the test has not touched the pre-chili craft.
- It must be run outside the favorable regime at least once. The pre-chili heats are fragile — volatile, time-limited, temperature-sensitive. Their failure modes are the evidence of what they are.
- It must produce something comparable to a reference, and where the observation is a judgment, the judges must not know which bowl is which. "Tastes right" is not an observation. "Fat-cutting rated equal to the control by two of three blinded tasters" is.
- It must be small enough that its failure is cheap. The purpose is to find out.

**Splitting claims.** A reconstruction carries several claims: *structural* (the function is served by this mechanism), *sensory* (it tastes like X), *historical* (this is what they did), and *contextual* (it is worth eating today). Test and record them separately. The normal outcome is that structural claims hold, sensory claims hold in part, historical claims hold only to the level the sources allow, and contextual claims are a matter of taste. A reconstruction whose historical claim is downgraded to "consistent with the sources, which are silent on procedure" is not a failure; it is honest.

**Feedback to Stage I.** A verdict is evidence about the mechanism. A sensory claim that fails because the effect could not be stored revises the candidate's "still present?" and "displaced" fields; a structural claim that holds for a candidate the sources never name is a prompt to search the materia medica again. Every verdict re-dates the strata.

**Exit criterion.** A fire record with the observation filled in and a verdict per claim.

## 10. Stage V — Table

### Serve it with the rupture named

**Principle.** A reconstruction that holds is still untrustworthy until it is presented with its own discontinuity on the plate. The two things an audience most wants — an unbroken lineage, or a clean debunking — are both wrong, and the honest statement is more interesting than either.

**Why reconstructors neglect this stage.** Whoever did the work sees the whole stratigraphy; the audience sees a dish and a date (§3.6, §3.7). The temptation is to let the dish carry a claim the stratigraphy cannot support ("this is what Ming Sichuan tasted like") or to overcorrect into dismissal ("Sichuan food is fake"). Both erase the finding, which is that a function older than the chili is served today by a mechanism that arrived after the present population did, and that this is exactly how traditions work.

**Table moves.**

1. **Lead with the function and its oldest date.** "Sichuan has loved pungent, fragrant food since at least the fourth century." Then: "What delivered it changed completely."
2. **Name the layers in order.** Cornel, ginger, Sichuan pepper → black pepper and mustard → chili. Population before and after the 1640s. Dishes by decade.
3. **State the entry function.** The chili came in as salt. Doubanjiang came in as thrift. The audience's model of why things exist is usually wrong in the same direction (they assume the current role was the original one); correct it explicitly.
4. **Report the breaker and the regime.** "It would have failed if blinded tasters had rated it below the control; they did not. It holds for a sauce made fresh and served within the half hour." The sentence that describes a test the reconstruction could have failed is the one that earns trust.
5. **Downgrade the historical claim to its evidence.** "Consistent with Ming sources, which give combination but not procedure; procedure supplied from the chemistry of the ingredients and from the Yuan mustard manual." Specialists trust bounded claims.
6. **Name the carrier as assumed.** "The appetite is attested among people who were then replaced; that it persisted by the place, rather than by the migrants' own pungent source cuisines, is the assumption here."
7. **Name the rupture as the point, not the problem.** The reconstruction of Sichuan after 1700 is not a flaw in the tradition; it is the tradition. Say that the migrants built it.

**The honesty clause.** Every claim that failed in Stage IV is stated as failed. If the pre-chili heat cannot be reproduced in a dish that depends on chili's storability, the dish is not reconstructed; a different dish is. The credibility of what held comes from the listing of what did not.

**Exit criterion.** A description a specialist in the target tradition can evaluate without having seen the work, containing the function, the strata, the entry functions, the breaker, the regime, the carrier assumed, and the failed claims.

## 11. The two clocks

Every tradition runs on two clocks, and most arguments about authenticity are arguments between people reading different ones.

The **slow clock** keeps the function: the appetite, the climate, the economic constraint, the body's demand. In the Sichuan case it is attested in the fourth century (§3.1) and still legible in the nineteenth; what carried it across the depopulation is the open question of §7, and the candidate carriers — the basin itself, a remnant, the migrants' already-pungent home cuisines — are all slow-clock entities.

The **fast clock** keeps the mechanism, the materials, the makers, and the recipes. It can be reset by a single catastrophe. It was reset in Sichuan between the 1640s and the 1680s (§3.7), and the dishes, most of the pantry, and the population all date from after the reset; the components that crossed — Sichuan pepper, ginger, soy ferments, vinegar, well salt — are the exceptions Stage I exists to find.

Afterheat assigns the clocks to stages deliberately:

| Stage | Dominant clock | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| I. Strata | Fast (dating mechanisms and makers) | Slow (dating the function and its carrier) |
| II. Appetite | Slow | — |
| III. Pantry | Fast (mechanisms by effect) | Slow (which effects serve the frozen function) |
| IV. Fire | Fast | Slow (the function clause is the pass criterion) |
| V. Table | Both, explicitly | — |

Two consequences follow.

**Authenticity is a slow-clock property; recipes are fast-clock artifacts.** A dish invented in 1862 can be fully authentic to a function attested in the 300s. A dish that reproduces a Ming ingredient list but serves a different function is a costume. The question "is this traditional?" has to be split before it can be answered.

**Do not read one clock with the other's precision.** Dating the function to the decade is false precision; dating mapo tofu to "ancient times" is false antiquity. Each layer is dated at the resolution its own clock supports.

## 12. The window rule

### A made effect has a schedule

Ground mustard seed is inert. Wet it and wait, and an enzyme converts a stored precursor into the volatile that burns. Boil it for long and the enzyme dies; chill it and the reaction stalls; leave it standing and the volatile escapes. The Yuan manual's instruction — scald briefly, set on cold ground, wait "a while," loosen with vinegar — is a schedule, and the heat exists only inside it.

That is the general lesson of every *made* effect (§3.3, §8): a procedural mechanism is not an ingredient list but a timetable — prepare, rest, serve — and a reconstruction that reproduces the ingredients without the window has not reproduced the mechanism. Three rules follow.

- **The regime carries a time axis.** The fire record's regime names preparation, rest, and serve times, not only temperatures and quantities.
- **The out-of-regime run is a run outside the window.** Boiling the mustard, or serving it an hour late, is the cheapest way to show which agent was doing the work and how brief its life was.
- **The historical claim is downgraded by the width of the window the sources leave open.** "After a while" admits anything from five minutes to an hour; the reconstruction's rest time is supplied from chemistry, and the Table says so.

A storable agent has no window, which is why it wins: it can be dosed at any hour, in any form, by anyone. That asymmetry — not superior flavor — is the mechanism by which the chili displaced the whole pre-chili pantry, and it is the asymmetry the inertness test exists to catch.

## 13. Working with machines

Language models are good at enumerating layers and poor at dating them. Asked for the history of Sichuan food, expect a fluent paragraph in which the chili is implied to be ancient, mapo tofu is "traditional," and the cornel does not appear — because that is the distribution of what has been written. The model tends to reproduce the smoothing that the framework exists to undo.

**Use them for Stage I enumeration and Stage III candidate generation.** Ask for every pungent agent attested in East Asian cooking before 1500, with active principles. Ask for every plant called *jiao*. Ask which receptors each principle acts on. These are retrieval tasks, and the output is a candidate list to be dated and verified, not a finding.

**Do not let them date, map, or taste.** A model given a Ming recipe will supply the missing ratios and temperatures with perfect fluency and no basis. It will produce a plausible alignment between any two ingredients because plausibility is what it optimizes. Dates come from sources; mappings come from chemistry; verdicts come from the fire. Keep the fire record away from the model until the observation is in: a model shown your prediction will help you rationalize the result.

**Hedge against flattening.** If every reconstructor asks the same model the same question, every reconstruction converges on the same smoothed past. Two hedges:

1. Seed the model with your own stratigraphy and ask what is *missing*, rather than asking it to start from zero.
2. Ask explicitly for displaced components — "what did this tradition stop using?" — because displaced components are underrepresented in the record the model learned from. Whether the cornel comes back is a cheap test of how much the model has flattened.

## 14. Metrics

Chosen because none can be raised by sounding more certain.

| Metric | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Stratum coverage** | Components with a dated, placed, sourced, confidence-marked card ÷ all components in the reconstruction (assumed-new components count in the denominator only) | 100% before Stage IV |
| **Function anteriority** | Years between the earliest attestation of the function and the earliest attestation of the current mechanism *at the same place*; counts only from a function attestation that has its own sourced card | Report it; a large positive number is the finding |
| **Displacement recall** | Agents named in the region's non-culinary sources (materia medica, product catalogues, gazetteers, ritual texts) for the role ÷ those with a stratum card | Rising |
| **Entry-function divergence** | Fraction of components whose entry function differs from their current function | Report it; expect it to be high |
| **Mapping depth** | Fraction of substitutions justified at the level of effect (receptor, volatility, timing) rather than name | 100% |
| **Breaker rate** | Fire records with a breaker that targets a function clause, written before cooking | 100% |
| **Out-of-regime rate** | Fire records with at least one deliberate run outside the regime | 100% |
| **Amendment log** | Function-statement changes after Stage II that carry a logged source ÷ all such changes | 100% |
| **Claim split** | Per reconstruction: structural / sensory / historical / contextual claims held | Record separately; expect structural > sensory > historical |
| **Rupture statement** | Does the Stage V description name what was lost, what was rebuilt, and the carrier assumed? (judged by a specialist) | Yes |

The one metric to avoid is **age claimed**. It rewards the cheapest move in the process and is inflated by every retelling.

## 15. Failure modes

| Failure | Signature | Stage where it should have been caught |
|---|---|---|
| **Present-pantry projection** | The current ingredient list is assumed to be the historical one; the chili reads as ancient | I — stratum cards |
| **Vocabulary antiquity** | A word (辛, 椒, "pepper," "hot") is treated as evidence that the current mechanism is old | I — first attestation of the mechanism, not the word |
| **Entry-function amnesia** | The current role is assumed to be the original one (chili as spice, not salt) | I — entry-function field |
| **Displacement blindness** | The replaced mechanism is not searched for because its successors never mention it | I — displaced field |
| **Makers-as-source** | Surviving hand-skill is used as primary evidence for pre-rupture procedure when the population was replaced | I — makers card |
| **Function tailoring** | A function clause is added or reshaped after the pantry is opened, to license a substitution | II — frozen statement; amendment log |
| **Carrier by assumption** | The function's persistence across the rupture is explained by the environment without the alternatives being named | II — carrier field |
| **Sensation anchoring** | A functionally sound reconstruction is rejected because it does not taste like the modern dish | II — function separated from sensation |
| **Name-matching** | Substitutes chosen because they share a name or category, not an effect | III — mechanism table, strip test |
| **Found-for-made swap** | A storable agent stands in for a procedural one and the change of craft goes unreported | III — procedure test |
| **Archaic garnish** | An old ingredient is added to a modern dish and the result is called a reconstruction | III — inertness test |
| **Chemistry breaker** | The only breaker written tests what the ingredient does in a textbook, not whether the function is served | IV — breaker targets a function clause |
| **Confident infill** | Missing ratios and procedures are filled from confidence rather than tested | IV — breaker required |
| **Deliciousness as proof** | The dish is good, so the interpretation must be right | IV — blinded control; split claims |
| **Regime creep** | An in-regime result is served as a general one | IV — out-of-regime run |
| **Unbroken-line narrative** | The rupture is smoothed into continuity; migrants become ancestors | V — rupture statement |
| **Debunker's overreach** | Because the mechanism is new, the tradition is declared fake; the slow clock is ignored | II — function statement; V |
| **Monoculture reconstruction** | Every reconstruction runs through the same model and converges on the same smoothed past | §13 |

## 16. A 30-day practice

**Week 1 — Strata.** Choose a tradition you care about. List every component of its current form that is treated as essential. Build a stratum card for each, including a place line and a carrier line for anything you believe crosses the rupture. Find the rupture, if there is one, and draw it. End the week by listing which components cross the line with a primary source and which do not. Expect the line to fall somewhere other than where you assumed.

**Week 2 — Appetite.** From the earliest sources, or from the environment if there are none, write the function statement with no mechanism nouns. Rewrite it three ways. Check that each clause disqualifies something. Name the carrier. Date the statement and freeze it. Show it to someone from an unrelated tradition and ask whether their tradition serves the same function. Note what they name.

**Week 3 — Pantry.** For the central role (in cooking, usually pungency, salt, or fat), build a mechanism table of every historical and candidate agent, characterized by effect. Apply the strip, procedure, and inertness tests against the frozen statement. If you find yourself wanting to change the statement, write the amendment and its source before you change anything.

**Week 4 — Fire and Table.** For the best-mapped substitution, write the fire record before touching an ingredient — breaker against a function clause, execution check for the made step, regime with its schedule, blinded control. Cook the smallest test, including one out-of-regime run. Record the claim split. Write a one-page Table description with the function, the strata, the entry functions, the breaker, the regime, the carrier assumed, and the failed claims. Give it to a specialist in the tradition and ask only: "Can you tell what was rebuilt, and what would break it?"

**End of month.** You have a stratigraphy, a frozen function statement, a mechanism table, and one substitution with a written breaker and an observation. That is the difference between a plausible story and a result.

## 17. Worked example

### A Ming-era heat without capsicum

**Strata.** Target: a sharp, warming sauce for rich braised pork, as a Sichuan household of the 1500s might have made it. Eleven components in the modern analog were carded. Two do not cross the rupture line: chili (first in Sichuan 1749) and doubanjiang (migrant; earliest documented firm 1803). Nine cross: Sichuan pepper (≥ fourth millennium BCE), ginger (pre-Han), small garlic (pre-Han), mustard (*Liji*; Yuan–Ming procedure), black pepper (third century, with a cost constraint), cornel (third-century *Fengtu Ji*; Song product catalogue; now absent from the cuisine), soy ferment (Han), vinegar (antiquity), salt (Sichuan brine wells). Coverage: 11 of 11 sourced; 2 of the 11 (mapo-era dates for the modern analog) rest on convention and are marked so. Makers: the population line is the 1640s–80s; the target predates it, so modern Sichuanese hand-skill is marked as not a primary source for procedure. One full card, for the richest entry:

```
COMPONENT: mustard paste (芥辣)

First attestation     Liji, Neize: "for fish slices, mustard sauce" (魚膾，芥醬). Procedure:
                      Jujia Biyong (Yuan); Songshi Yangsheng Bu (1504).
Place attested        Zhou/Han ritual texts (north China); Yuan–Ming household manuals
                      (Jiangnan). No Sichuan-specific attestation of the procedure.
Entry route           Native (Brassica juncea, domesticated in China).
Entry function        Condiment for raw fish; cutting richness.
Adoption lag          None recorded.
Displaced             Nothing recorded; later displaced in Sichuan by the chili.
Still present?        Residual — mustard survives in Sichuan cooking as a minor condiment.
Carrier across        Texts (the Yuan–Ming manuals); not place and not makers.
the rupture
Confidence            Attestation and procedure primary; Sichuan use in the 1500s inferred
                      from national practice, not from a Sichuan source.
```

**Appetite.** Function statement, frozen before the pantry was opened: "Deliver a sharp, aromatic, warming stimulus that cuts fat, at household cost, in a form that can be made daily." Carrier assumed: texts, for mustard; place, for the appetite as a whole — with the migrants' source cuisines named as the live alternative. Three rewrites: (a) "cut richness" — retrieves vinegar and mustard; (b) "warm against damp" — retrieves ginger, pepper, cassia; (c) "stimulate sharply and fade" — retrieves mustard, garlic, cornel. **Amendment, logged:** "made daily" tightened to "made fresh for the meal," licensed by the Yuan–Ming mustard procedure (§3.3), which describes a condiment prepared at the point of use. Logged before Stage III.

**Pantry.** Role: pungency. Incumbent (modern): chili — non-volatile TRPV1, oil-borne, slow onset, long duration, found. Candidates: mustard — volatile TRPA1, water-released, fast onset, short duration, *made*; ginger — TRPV1, moderate, intensified by drying, found; cornel — *Zanthoxylum* alkylamides, tingling-pungent, found, no longer obtainable as a culinary product; black pepper — TRPV1/TRPA1, moderate, found, costly. Strip test: no single candidate coincides with chili; the *combination* mustard + ginger + Sichuan pepper covers nasal sharpness, oral warmth, and tingling, a wider sensory field than chili alone with a shorter half-life. Procedure test: mustard is made — the reconstruction's craft is in the mustard step. Inertness test: chili's storability is not inert for modern dishes but *is* inert for a sauce made fresh for the meal; the logged amendment is what licenses the substitution, and the Table says so.

**Fire.** Prediction, targeting the clauses "cuts fat" and "sharp": a mustard-ginger-Sichuan-pepper sauce, mustard wetted with lukewarm water and rested covered 10–15 minutes, cut with vinegar, served within 30 minutes, will be rated by blinded tasters as cutting the fat of braised pork at least as well as a chili control. Breaker: if the blinded panel rates fat-cutting below the control, the mapping fails at "cuts fat." Execution check: nasal heat within 15 seconds, gone within 2 minutes; tingling persisting 5–10 minutes; the boiled bowl shows no nasal heat. Regime: water below ~50°C; rest 10–15 minutes; serve within 30 minutes. Out-of-regime run: boil the mustard. Control: a chili-oil sauce on the same pork; three tasters, bowls coded, order randomized. Smallest cook: three small bowls, one braised pork, two hours.

**Observation (as recorded).** In-regime: nasal heat at ~10 seconds, gone by ~90 seconds; tingle persisted; fat-cutting rated equal to the control by two blinded tasters, better by one. Boiled: nasal heat absent; warmth and tingle present; fat-cutting rated below the control by all three. Chili control: heat still building at 2 minutes. Verdict per claim: structural — holds; sensory — holds for sharpness and tingle, does not reproduce the lasting burn (and was not predicted to); historical — consistent with Ming combination, procedure supplied from mustard chemistry and the Yuan manual, Sichuan use inferred rather than attested; contextual — two tasters would order it again. Feedback to Stage I: the boiled bowl's failure confirms mustard's "still present? — residual" entry: an agent with a half-hour window could not have survived the move to a storable pantry.

**Table.** Written for a Sichuan cook: "A fresh mustard-ginger-pepper sauce for fatty pork, in the family of sharp sauces used in China before the sea pepper arrived. It is brief and nasal rather than deep and lasting; that difference is the difference between a heat made for the meal and a heat kept in a jar. It holds only when the mustard is wetted warm, rested a quarter hour, and served within the half hour; boiled or kept, it dies. It would have failed if tasters who did not know which bowl was which had rated it below the chili sauce; they did not. The Ming texts give the combination but not the method; the method here comes from the Yuan manual and from how mustard works, and no Sichuan text of the 1500s describes it. The chili itself came to this province as a salt substitute, not a spice. The dish you know — the bean paste, the red oil — was built by families who came here after 1700. That the appetite they served was the one the fourth-century gazetteer describes is the assumption here; the alternative is that they brought it with them from Hunan."

## 18. Scope

### When the framework applies

Afterheat is a method for a specific situation, and it degrades outside it. Three conditions must hold:

1. **There is a rupture.** A continuous tradition with a complete record needs ordinary history, not reconstruction.
2. **The function is attested before the rupture, independently of the current mechanism** — or it can be inferred from the environment and is marked as inferred. Without this, Stage II has nothing to freeze, the method collapses to Stage I plus a plausible story, and it should say so.
3. **The function can be tested.** A function that cannot be put to a breaker (a belief, a meaning, a status) can be stratified and mapped but not fired; the result is a bounded historical claim, not a reconstruction.

Where all three hold, the questions are the same in every domain: how old is the function, how young is the mechanism, were the makers replaced, and by what did the function cross. The answer, whatever it is, is stated with the rupture in it.

### Where else the structure recurs

**A codebase after the authors leave.** Fast clock reset; the slow clock is the business function the code served. Present-pantry projection: assuming the current architecture is the original design. Entry-function amnesia: a module that now reads as central was added as a workaround. Strip test: describe each component by what it *does* to data, not by its name. Found/made: a behavior that is produced by a running process (a cache warmed on a schedule, an index rebuilt nightly) versus one stored in the code. Fire: a test with a breaker written against the business function before refactoring. Table: a document that says what was rebuilt, what the original authors are not here to confirm, and which behaviors are preserved by inference.

**A craft whose material went extinct.** Function: what the material did (flex, grain, weight under load). Pantry: candidates by property, not by appearance — a modern material that gives the look without the working behavior is name-matching. Found/made: a property produced by working the material (tempering, seasoning, curing) versus one stored in the stock; a substitute stock that skips the working has changed the craft. Fire: a piece made to be used, with a predicted failure point. Table: "this is what the object did; this is how the new material does it; this is where it will not."

**A rebuilt institution.** The slow clock is the need the institution met; the fast clock is its charter, staff, and procedures. Unbroken-line narrative: founders' names on a body that was reconstituted twice. Debunker's overreach: "it isn't the real X" — but the need is the same and the new body serves it. Carrier: by what did the need persist — the population it served, the place, the texts? Rupture statement: the dates of the breaks, printed in the history, not elided.

## 19. Glossary

**Adoption lag** — the time between a mechanism's availability at a place and its recorded use there. **Afterheat** — the function that continues after the mechanism that delivered it is gone; the residual signal a reconstruction searches for. **Amendment log** — the record of any change to the frozen function statement, with the source that licenses it. **Appetite** — Stage II; the function statement with no mechanism nouns, frozen and dated. **Breaker** — the observation, written before testing against a clause of the function, that would falsify a reconstruction. **Carrier** — the thing by which a function is claimed to have crossed a rupture: place, remnant population, migrants' source traditions, or texts. **Claim split** — separate recording of structural, sensory, historical, and contextual claims and their outcomes. **Displaced component** — a mechanism replaced by a successor and typically unrecorded by it (the food cornel). **Entry function** — the role a component first served when it arrived, as distinct from its current role (the chili as salt). **Entry route** — how a component arrived: native, trade, migration, accident. **Execution check** — the observation that confirms a made step was performed; necessary, but not a breaker. **Fast clock** — the rate of change of mechanisms, materials, makers, and recipes; resettable by catastrophe. **Fire** — Stage IV; the test with a prediction and breaker written first. **Fire record** — the written prediction, breaker, execution check, regime, out-of-regime run, control, and verdict for one test. **Found / made** — whether an effect is stored in an ingredient or produced by technique at the moment of use. **Function anteriority** — the time between the earliest attestation of the function and that of the current mechanism at the same place. **Inertness test** — verifying that properties of the incumbent not reproduced by a substitute did not matter to the frozen function. **Jiao** (椒) — the Chinese word that covers Sichuan pepper, black pepper, and chili; the central example of vocabulary hiding three mechanisms. **Mechanism table** — the characterization of each candidate agent by effect rather than name. **Out-of-regime run** — a deliberate test outside the window in which the mechanism is expected to work. **Pantry** — Stage III; the mapping of mechanisms by effect. **Plausible story** — a reconstruction that does not yet meet all five conditions of §2. Where every real reconstruction begins. **Procedure test** — distinguishing made effects from found ones. **Regime** — the conditions, including the schedule, under which a prediction is claimed to hold. **Rupture** — a break in the fast clock: lost makers, materials, texts, or supply. **Slow clock** — the rate of change of the function; kept by the place, the body, or the carrier more than by any one generation of makers. **Smallest cook** — the minimum preparation that produces the observation a fire record needs. **Sound** — the state of a reconstruction that meets all five conditions of §2. **Stratum card** — the dated, placed, sourced record of a single component's first attestation, entry route, entry function, displacement, and carrier. **Strata** — Stage I; the stratigraphy of components across the rupture line. **Strip test** — replacing plant and ingredient names with active principles and effects to check whether a substitution holds. **Table** — Stage V; the presentation of a reconstruction with its rupture, regime, and carrier named. **Window rule** — that a made effect exists only in a schedule, and that its reconstruction and its test must carry that schedule. **Xin** (辛) — the pre-chili Chinese category of pungency, covering a family of sensations rather than a single burn.
